# Grading, mastery and XP

All three are computed in the main process from stored records (`src/core/engine/*`), so any number can be recomputed from the database.

## Course grade (`grading.ts`)

| Category | Weight | What counts |
|---|---|---|
| Lesson quizzes | 30% | Best attempt per lesson. A passed Show What You Know (≥ 85%) counts as that lesson's quiz. |
| Unit assessments | 45% | Best of the **first two** attempts per unit. |
| Semester assessment | 20% | Best attempt. |
| Practice completion | 5% | Share of started instructional lessons whose independent practice was completed (counted as soon as the practice set is complete; a tested-out lesson counts as complete). Review, assessment and project days have no independent practice and are not part of it. The lesson being worked on right now is left out until its practice is done, so opening a lesson never lowers the grade; a lesson left unfinished while the student moved on to another day (one started after it, in any course order) counts as incomplete. A lesson completed by passing its quiz without doing its practice (possible since open access, by jumping to the quiz) counts as complete, like a test-out: the passed quiz shows the skill. Accuracy in practice never lowers the grade. |

- Unit reviews, the checkpoint, cumulative and semester reviews, and the three capstone projects (days 85-87) are not graded: they give practice with hints and show which skills to review. Their answers still count toward skill mastery, like any practice.
- The diagnostic (placement check) is not graded. Its answers count toward skill mastery as diagnostic evidence (×1.5), and it can suggest Show What You Know lessons and warm-up practice; see below.
- Categories with no work yet are left out and the remaining weights are rescaled (e.g. quizzes only = 100% quizzes).
- Practice completion alone never produces a grade.
- Letters: A ≥ 90, B ≥ 80, C ≥ 70, below 70 F. Percentages are rounded to one decimal.
- Grade trend (Parent Dashboard): one point per day with graded work, each computed from the work and the practice completion as of that moment, plus a point for today whenever the grade has changed since; the last point always equals the current grade.
- Course map: a unit assessment shows its graded score (best of the first two attempts); the semester assessment its best attempt. A third or later unit-assessment attempt is a practice retake: it is labelled "practice retake, not graded" and never changes the shown score.

## Lesson flow and passing

- Quiz pass: 80% (per-lesson setting). Quizzes use deferred feedback and no hints.
- Saving a quiz, Show What You Know or assessment answer: an answer that cannot be read, or that is not written in the form the question asks for (e.g. slope-intercept instead of standard form, an exact √61 on a "round to the nearest tenth" question, an unsimplified 3 + 4), is **not saved**. The student sees what to fix (the message is the same whether the value is right or wrong, so it gives nothing away), and the question stays unanswered until a readable answer in the requested form is saved. A correct value in the wrong form is therefore never graded wrong unless the student chooses to keep it: saving the same answer a second time keeps it as written (so no one is ever stuck on a question), and it is then graded as written. Re-saving over a saved answer with a typo keeps the earlier saved answer and says so. A refused answer stays in the answer box with its message (also after moving to another question and back), and the question counts as answered only once the main process has saved an answer. A known misconception answer (e.g. "y < -5" read off the wrong axis of an x-inequality graph) is a readable wrong answer: it is saved and graded with its targeted feedback, not refused as unreadable; a value-independent form rule (e.g. "write it completely factored") still applies to it like to any answer. For answers in thousands of dollars only an amount written in dollars ($2,000 for 2) is the wrong form, the same rule the checker uses; "$2", "$2 thousand" and an answer of 1,500 (thousand dollars) are saved as written.
- A failed quiz leads to feedback, corrections, Teach Me Again and targeted practice on the missed skills, then a retake with new questions. Retakes are unlimited; the best score counts. When a missed skill rests on a prerequisite that is not yet Proficient (and was not itself missed), the targeted practice starts with a short refresher: up to 2 such prerequisites (Learning/Developing first, then Not started), each with its teaching lesson's introductory worked example and one level-1 problem, before the missed-skill problems (`remediationRefreshers` in `lessons.ts`).
- Results pages name strengths and next steps: "You did well on" lists the skills answered at least 2/3 right (or, when none reaches that, those at least half right), so a 4/6 quiz with one miss per skill still names its strengths; "Needs practice" lists the rest. The summary (did well, needs practice, next step) is stored with each graded quiz/assessment (`assessments.summary_json`, schema v2), so a past result shows the same next step it showed then; results graded before v2 (v0.16) rebuild it from their stored items with the same functions that write it (`summarizeSkills`, `nextStepFor` in `core/engine/results.ts`), and the results page of that day or lesson shows the row's summary too (`withRowSummary`), so it and the Parent Dashboard's assessment detail always show the same wording.
- A passed Show What You Know shows its results first (score, each missed item with its correction, the next step) and the summary afterwards.
- A unit review day becomes **optional** (labelled "Optional: you already showed these skills" on the course map) when every lesson of its unit is completed or tested out and every skill it reviews is at least Proficient; "Continue learning" and the course map's "Suggested next" marker skip it. Assessments are never optional. For progress an optional review counts as done whether or not it is taken (`isDoneForProgress` in `src/shared/progress.ts`: completed, tested out, or an optional review): the current unit and week, semester %, pace, Coming up, milestones, the weekly report's next lesson, the course map's days-done count and both dashboards all use it, so skipping the review moves the student and parent views on to the next unit together and the semester can reach 100%.
- **Open access** (owner request, 2026-10-13; on for every student, no parent setting): every course day (lessons, unit reviews, the checkpoint and cumulative review, unit assessments, capstone projects, the semester review and the semester assessment) can be opened, started and completed at any time, in any order. There are no unlock rules: no "previous lesson finished" rule, no honest-attempt rule, no review-before-assessment rule, and no server-side refusal based on the course sequence (only a day without content in this version cannot be opened). The course map marks the **suggested next day**, the first day not done for progress (`isDoneForProgress`; an optional review counts as done), and "Continue learning" on the home screen goes there (resuming it if it is in progress; another day in progress is offered as "Also in progress"). Work done out of order counts exactly as in order: a unit assessment taken before its lessons is graded and counted like any other (best of the first two attempts), and lessons, reviews and projects complete normally. One-time XP awards are paid once per day whatever the order. States saved by older versions may still hold an honest-attempt count; it is ignored.
- Inside a lesson the sections still run in order with **Continue** (teach, then practice, then the quiz), and any section can also be opened from the section list. Feedback and the mastery check open once a quiz has been graded (before that they have nothing to show), and the lesson is finished from its summary only after a passed quiz. Reaching the quiz without finishing guided practice shows a tip suggesting guided practice first; it never blocks the quiz.
- Closed book, inside the attempt: a quiz or Show What You Know is in progress from the moment its questions are shown until it is graded, and a unit or semester assessment from when its questions are shown until it is graded (the overview page alone does not count). Inside the quiz, Show What You Know or assessment there are no hints (refused by the main process), the lesson view carries no pages or worked examples, and Teach Me Again is refused. The student may **leave** at any time (open access): to the lesson's own pages or practice, to another lesson, to Teach Me Again from the lesson pages, or to a review or project day. The attempt stays saved where it was left, and going back resumes the same questions with the saved answers. Each place visited while an attempt is in progress is noted on that attempt (for example "Graphing Linear Functions: Worked Examples", "Slope: opened the lesson", "... : Teach Me Again") and stored with its graded row (`assessments.summary_json.leftNotes`; no schema change). The results page and the Parent Dashboard's assessment detail list these visits ("Left during this attempt to view ..."), and the assessment history shows "left to view lessons (N)". It is information for the parent, never a penalty: the score and grade are computed exactly as before.
- Show What You Know: the quiz plus two harder items, no hints, 85% to pass. Failing it has no penalty; the student learns the lesson normally. It is offered (on the course map too) for any lesson not completed or tested out, in any order, until the first quiz attempt or a finished test-out, also when the lesson has been opened; starting it again before finishing resumes the same questions. (The "before the first quiz attempt" rule is kept: it is not about the course order, it stops a test-out from replacing a quiz the student has already started; Show What You Know remains the way to skip a lesson with credit.)
- Quiz and assessment times are active time: each gap between two actions counts at most 10 minutes and a gap that runs backwards (clock changed) counts zero, so closing the app overnight mid-quiz adds at most 10 minutes and a time is never negative. Results are saved to disk immediately.

## Skill mastery (`mastery.ts`)

Each skill's state is replayed from all counted answers in time order (deterministic).

- Score: weighted average of the last 12 answers. Newer answers weigh more (×0.85 per step back). Quizzes ×1.5, assessments and test-outs ×2, independent practice ×1, remediation ×0.7, guided ×0.5. Credit is reduced by hints (−0.15 per hint level, minimum 0.3) and retries (0.6).
- Learning: fewer than 3 answers or score < 0.5 (a diagnostic answer counts as 1.5 answers here, so two correct placement answers are enough to set a starting stage). Developing: score < 0.75 or fewer than 2 independent correct answers. Proficient: score ≥ 0.75 with ≥ 2 independent correct answers.
- **Mastered:** score ≥ 0.88, ≥ 6 answers, ≥ 4 independent correct answers, a correct quiz/assessment answer, **and independent correct work spanning at least 20 hours** (a later study day), so mastery means retained, not just done once.
- Drops are proportional: answers less than an hour apart form one sitting (a quiz, an assessment, a practice session), and within one sitting a skill falls at most one stage below where the sitting started, unless the sitting holds 3 or more misses on that skill. So two easy misses on one assessment take a Mastered skill to Proficient, not to Learning.
- A mastered skill drops when its score falls below 0.7 **and** at least 2 of the answers since it was mastered (within the last 12) were misses. One miss never removes it, even on a unit assessment or an easy quiz item. It also drops, whatever the number of misses, when its score falls below the Developing threshold (0.5): a student who gets every answer right only after the full set of hints has not kept mastery.
- Spaced review: Proficient/Mastered skills come back after 2, 4, 7, 14, 30 days; review items are mixed into later practice (any lesson's independent practice can review any skill the student has already practiced, whatever the course order, other than the skills that lesson teaches). The first review is 2 days after the skill reaches Proficient. Only an answer given once the skill is due counts as a review: an independent correct one (first try, no hints, not guided or remediation work, the same rule as "independent" above) moves to the next interval, a miss brings the skill back the next day and restarts the ladder, and a correct answer that needed hints or a second try leaves the skill due (it keeps coming back until it is answered independently). Independent correct practice before the due date keeps the current interval, counted from that latest work; hinted or retried correct practice does not move the date; a miss before then also brings it back the next day.

## Graph answers (`graphAnswer.ts`)

Some problems ask the student to draw a graph from a situation (A.PAR.4.1, A.FGR.7.6, A.PAR.8.3, and linear models in A.FGR.2.2): they type a label for each axis, choose what one grid square is worth on each axis, place points on grid intersections (mouse, or arrow keys and Enter), and for an inequality choose a solid or dashed boundary and the side to shade. The main process grades the drawing exactly (rational arithmetic) in this order; the renderer never receives the target, the accepted scales or the label words.

1. Not finished (no scale or label chosen, too few points, a point off the grid, boundary or shading not chosen): not graded; the student is told what is missing.
2. Labels: each axis label must name its own quantity and must not name the other axis's quantity (a label naming both quantities fits neither axis). Each situation lists the words for its quantities, with stems for inflections ("tutor*" for tutor, tutored, tutoring; "babysit*" and "babysat"; "sketch*"/"book*"; cost, total, money, dollars for a cost; length/duration for a rental time). Unit words (feet, ft, sq ft, hours, $, mg, ...) never count as naming a quantity unless the situation lists that unit as a name for it (e.g. "dollars" for a cost, "hours" for a time, "square feet" for an area), so "Area (sq ft)" never passes as a height. Units: a label without a unit is fine; a label with a unit the quantity is not measured in ("Time (minutes)" when the time is in hours, "Paint sets ($)" for a count) is wrong with a message naming the right unit. Swapped labels get their own message.
3. Scales: an axis must reach everything the situation needs, the graph must use at least a quarter of the axis, and the key values (starting value, intercepts, vertex) must land on grid lines. Every accepted pair of scales is checked to be drawable when the problem is generated.
4. Points: every point exactly on the line or curve. A parabola also needs its vertex and a point on each side; an exponential needs its starting value.
5. Inequality: solid for ≤/≥, dashed for </>; shading on the side that holds the solutions.

Misconceptions for graphs (swapped slope and intercept, a line through the origin, the wrong direction, swapped intercepts, flipped shading, solid vs dashed, a peak at the second zero, linear instead of exponential growth, swapped labels) are recognized when the drawing is a correct drawing of the misconceived answer, and give targeted feedback. Results pages describe a drawn answer in words.

## Teach Me Again as a support signal

Teach Me Again is never penalized. When a student opens it 3 or more times in one lesson, the Parent Dashboard ("Support signals", all time) and the weekly report (uses within that week) note it as a support signal ("... Asking again is a good habit, not a problem"), so a parent can check in; it never changes a grade, XP or mastery.

## Diagnostic / placement check (`diagnostic.ts`)

Offered after the first profile is created and on the home screen until it is taken; a parent can skip it (or offer it again) with the Parent PIN.

- Part 1 checks the 12 earlier-grade prerequisite skills; Part 2 probes 8 key course skills (S1.02, S1.05, S1.07, S1.09, S1.12, S2.01, S3.03, S5.01).
- One question at a time, no hints, no right/wrong feedback. "I haven't learned this yet" marks the skill not learned and records no wrong answer. An answer that cannot be read or is not in the requested form is not scored: a neutral message says what to fix and the question stays open, with the answer still in the box. As in quizzes, submitting the same wrong-form answer a second time scores it as written (an unreadable one never is), and "I haven't learned this yet" is always a way forward.
- Evidence rule (one slip never decides a skill): a prerequisite is shown by a first correct answer; after a miss, two misses mean it needs a refresh, and a miss then a correct answer goes to a third question. A course skill needs two correct answers; two misses mean it is still to learn; a split goes to a third question. At most 3 questions per skill.
- Starting mastery: the answers count as diagnostic evidence, so a course skill shown with two correct answers starts at Proficient (never Mastered, which needs quiz/assessment work on more than one day), and a skill with two misses stays Learning. A stage set by the diagnostic alone gives no milestone XP; that is earned in lessons.
- Results: each skill's verdict, a suggested starting day (the first day not done; every day is open), Show What You Know suggestions for lessons whose course skill was shown (marked on the course map), and optional warm-up practice (hints, worked solutions) on prerequisites that need a refresh.

## XP (`xp.ts`)

- Correct answer: base by activity (guided 5, remediation 6, independent/review 10, quiz 15, assessment 20) × difficulty (1, 1.25, 1.5); second try ×0.5, later tries ×0.25; hints −20% per level (minimum 40%); multiple choice ×0.5. In guided steps, tries are counted per step: a slip on an earlier step does not make the final step a "second try". An answer sent again for a guided step that is already done (a double click) is ignored, not graded against the next step.
- **No XP** for an answer flagged as guessing: a retry within 4 seconds of the previous wrong try (timed by the main process from when that feedback was given), or 3 wrong answers within 30 seconds. Quiz, assessment and test-out answers are graded all at once when the set is finished, so they are left out of the "3 wrong in 30 seconds" count; corrections made right after a quiz are not called guessing.
- One-time awards: lesson complete 50, quiz pass 25 (+25 at ≥ 90%), test-out 75, skill Proficient 20, skill Mastered 40.
- Retakes cannot farm XP: a quiz or assessment retake pays answer XP only for improving on the best earlier attempt (the total paid for a lesson's quiz answers equals its best attempt), and a unit/semester assessment retake after a pass pays no answer XP. Remediation and correction answers earn at most 60 XP per lesson or day in total, below the pass bonuses, so failing on purpose never earns more than passing.
- Levels: total XP for level L is 50·L·(L−1) (L2 = 100, L3 = 300, L4 = 600 …).

## Streaks (`dates.ts`)

A day counts (local calendar date) when the student resolves at least 5 problems (wrong tries and answers flagged as guessing do not count), studies at least 10 active **learning** minutes, or completes a piece of practice work: a guided or independent practice set, a quiz, or a review/assessment/project day (reading lesson pages such as the goal, instruction or examples does not count on its own). Active time counts only while the window is visible and the student has interacted in the last 90 seconds (measured with a monotonic clock, so changing the system clock cannot credit idle time). One heartbeat timer runs for the whole session, so moving between screens does not drop time.

Learning time is time in a lesson (any section), on a review/assessment/project day, in the diagnostic, or in practice (`LEARNING_ACTIVITIES` in `src/shared/api.ts`). Time on the dashboard, course map, skills list, settings, the profile picker or Parent Mode is not learning time: those screens send no heartbeat, and the main process ignores any non-learning activity, so it never adds study minutes and never makes a streak day. Every minute total (today, this week, the weekly report, all time, both dashboards) reads the same learning-time records, so the student and parent numbers always agree; time an older version recorded on non-learning screens is left out of all of them.

Minutes are rounded per day and every total adds up days, so today's, each week's and the all-time minutes always agree. A weekly report shows the current streak for this week; for a past week, the streak as it stood at the end of that week's last study day.
